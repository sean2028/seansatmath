1. Standard Quadratic Form

\[y=ax^2+bx+c\]

This is the most common form on the SAT.

Opening

\(a>0\): parabola opens up.

\(a<0\): parabola opens down.

Intercept Shortcut

The y-intercept is \((0,c)\), because when \(x=0\), \(y=c\).

Very useful SAT shortcut: \(a+b+c=f(1)\), because \(f(1)=a(1)^2+b(1)+c\).

2. Vertex Formula from Standard Form

For:

\[y=ax^2+bx+c\]

The x-coordinate of the vertex is:

\[x=-\frac{b}{2a}\]

Then substitute that x-value into the equation to get the y-coordinate.

\[\text{Vertex}=\left(-\frac{b}{2a},\,f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)\right)\]

Example

\[y=x^2-6x+5\]

Here \(a=1\) and \(b=-6\).

\[x=-\frac{-6}{2(1)}=3\]
\[y=3^2-6(3)+5=-4\]
\[\text{Vertex}=(3,-4)\]

3. Vertex Form

\[y=a(x-h)^2+k\]
\[\text{Vertex}=(h,k)\]

This is the best form when the problem gives a vertex.

Sign Warning

\((x-3)^2\) means the vertex x-value is \(3\), not \(-3\).

Sign Warning

\((x+4)^2\) means the vertex x-value is \(-4\).

Examples

\[y=2(x-3)^2-5\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \text{Vertex}=(3,-5)\]
\[y=a(x+4)^2+7\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \text{Vertex}=(-4,7)\]

4. Factored Form / Intercept Form

\[y=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)\]

The x-intercepts are \((r_1,0)\) and \((r_2,0)\).

Example

\[y=2(x-1)(x-5)\]

The x-intercepts are \((1,0)\) and \((5,0)\).

The axis of symmetry is halfway between the roots:

\[x=\frac{r_1+r_2}{2}=\frac{1+5}{2}=3\]

5. Sum and Product of Roots — Vieta’s Formulas

To avoid confusing the general coefficients with problem parameters such as \(a\) and \(b\), write the general quadratic using capital letters:

\[Ax^2+Bx+C=0\]
Sum of Roots
\[r_1+r_2=-\frac{B}{A}\]
Product of Roots
\[r_1r_2=\frac{C}{A}\]
What the question asksFastest method
Sum of the solutions\(-\frac{B}{A}\)
Product of the solutions\(\frac{C}{A}\)
Actual solutionsFactor or use the quadratic formula
Number of real solutionsUse the discriminant \(B^2-4AC\)
Important: A constant does not have to be a known number. A parameter such as \(a\) or \(b\) can remain symbolic while you solve for \(x\).

Factoring with parameters

\[ mx^2+(mq+p)x+pq =mx^2+mqx+px+pq =(mx+p)(x+q) \]

Therefore, the roots are:

\[x=-\frac{p}{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x=-q\]

6. Quadratic Formula

Use this when you need to solve:

\[ax^2+bx+c=0\]
\[x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\]

This gives the roots or x-intercepts of the parabola.

7. Discriminant

\[b^2-4ac\]

It tells you how many real x-intercepts the parabola has.

DiscriminantMeaning
\(b^2-4ac>0\)Two real solutions; the parabola crosses the x-axis twice.
\(b^2-4ac=0\)One repeated real solution; the parabola touches the x-axis at the vertex.
\(b^2-4ac<0\)No real solutions; the parabola does not touch the x-axis.

8. Completing the Square

This converts standard form into vertex form.

Example

\[y=x^2-8x+3\]
\[ y=(x^2-8x)+3 =(x^2-8x+16)-16+3 =(x-4)^2-13 \]
\[\text{Vertex}=(4,-13)\]
SAT often tests completing the square because it reveals the vertex.

9. Maximum and Minimum Value

\[y=a(x-h)^2+k\]
\(a>0\)

The parabola opens up, so the vertex is a minimum.

Minimum value \(=k\).

\(a<0\)

The parabola opens down, so the vertex is a maximum.

Maximum value \(=k\).

10. Axis of Symmetry

FormAxis of symmetry
\(y=ax^2+bx+c\)\(x=-\frac{b}{2a}\)
\(y=a(x-h)^2+k\)\(x=h\)
\(y=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)\)\(x=\frac{r_1+r_2}{2}\)

11. Circle Equation

\[(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\]

Center: \((h,k)\)

Radius: \(r\)

Example

\[(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25\]

Center: \((3,-2)\); radius: \(5\).

12. Ellipse Equation

\[\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1\]

Center: \((h,k)\). The larger denominator gives the longer radius direction.

Example

\[\frac{(x-2)^2}{25}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{9}=1\]

Center: \((2,-1)\); horizontal radius \(5\); vertical radius \(3\).

13. Hyperbola Equation

Less common on the SAT, but still possible.

Horizontal hyperbola

\[\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1\]

Vertical hyperbola

\[\frac{(y-k)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}=1\]
Circle and ellipse use a plus sign; a hyperbola uses a minus sign.

15. Best SAT Decision Strategy

When you see a quadratic question, first ask: What exactly is being requested?

  • Given a vertex → use \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\).
  • Given roots / x-intercepts → use \(y=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)\).
  • Asked for the sum of roots → use \(-\frac{B}{A}\).
  • Asked for the product of roots → use \(\frac{C}{A}\).
  • Asked for the actual roots → factor or use the quadratic formula.
  • Asked for the number of real roots → use \(b^2-4ac\).
  • Asked about \(a+b+c\) → recognize \(a+b+c=f(1)\).
  • When parameters appear, treat them as fixed numbers unless the problem asks you to solve for them.

Practice 4, Q19 Pattern Recap

Problem type: product of roots with symbolic parameters.

\[57x^2+(57b+a)x+ab=0\]

Fast method: Vieta’s formula

\[ A=57,\qquad B=57b+a,\qquad C=ab \]
\[ r_1r_2=\frac{C}{A}=\frac{ab}{57} \]

The problem says the product equals \(kab\), so:

\[ kab=\frac{ab}{57} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad k=\frac{1}{57} \]

Secondary method: factor with parameters

\[ 57x^2+(57b+a)x+ab =57x^2+57bx+ax+ab =(57x+a)(x+b) \]
\[ x=-\frac{a}{57} \qquad\text{or}\qquad x=-b \]
Answer: \(\boxed{\frac{1}{57}}\)

Practice 4, Q22 Pattern Recap

Problem type: vertex given; asks for \(a+b+c\).

  1. Recognize \(a+b+c=f(1)\).
  2. Vertex \((9,-14)\) gives \(f(x)=A(x-9)^2-14\).
  3. Two x-intercepts and a vertex below the x-axis mean the parabola opens upward, so \(A>0\).
\[ f(1)=A(1-9)^2-14=64A-14 \]

Since \(A>0\), \(f(1)>-14\). Among the choices, only \(-12\) works.

Answer: \(\boxed{-12}\)
